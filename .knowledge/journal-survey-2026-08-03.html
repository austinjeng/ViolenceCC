1 · The recommendation

Two facts reshape the decision before any journal is named.

Fact one

You have no deadline. The NTUST Information Management graduate regulations — both currently-applicable versions — contain zero occurrences of 期刊, 發表, or SCI. There is no journal-publication requirement for the master's degree, and your oral defence already passed. This submission is for merit and career value, not a graduation gate, so you can afford to aim high and ladder down.

Fact two

Publishing costs you nothing at most of the good targets. Every Elsevier and Springer hybrid journal offers a subscription route that Elsevier states verbatim as “No publication fee charged to authors.” The venues that actually cost money — IEEE Access, MDPI, IET, PeerJ — are the fully open-access ones. Cost should therefore not drive your choice among the free options.

Given that, the ladder below maximises expected outcome rather than speed. Steps 1–3 are all Elsevier, so a rejection at one carries your reviews to the next through the Article Transfer Service instead of restarting review.

First

Image and Vision Computing Target

Elsevier · JIF 5.0 · Q2 in Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence (67/210) · costs you $0 · 16-day first decision, 167 days to acceptance

The heaviest recent publisher of your exact problem among realistic venues — roughly nine genuine VAD papers in 2024–2026, including weakly supervised and audio-visual work. Its scope statement explicitly rewards quantitative performance evaluation and comparison, which is what your paper actually is. A failed attempt costs about two weeks.

Second

Computer Vision and Image Understanding Target

Elsevier · JIF 3.6 · Q2 in CS-AI (99/210) · $0 · 7-day first decision (aggressive desk screen)

The only venue whose aims-and-scope explicitly solicits “papers offering insights that differ from predominant views” — a clause written for your TENT/SAR null result. It has already published a weakly supervised VAD paper presented as a baseline rather than a SOTA claim, at 85.30% UCF-Crime AUC. Promote this to first place if — and only if — you can instrument the LayerNorm head and turn “AUC moves <0.1pp” into a measured mechanism.

Third

Journal of Visual Communication and Image Representation Safety net

Elsevier · JIF 2.8 · Q2 in CS-Software Engineering (59/128), Q3 in CS-Information Systems · $0 · 215 days to acceptance, the slowest here

The highest probability of publication anywhere on this list. It published a 2025 paper that builds its own multiply-distorted video dataset, analyses how data-quality fluctuation degrades VAD methods, and makes no leaderboard claim at all — structurally the same paper as your UCF-Crime-C work. Reframed robustness-first, this is where the thesis most likely ends up in print.

The framing principle

Three assets are publishable regardless of the operating point, and none of them is the fusion architecture: (a) a controlled four-backbone comparison under one fixed head, one protocol and matched seeds — nobody has published this for weakly supervised VAD, and your own finding that the SO400M lead on XD-Violence was seed noise is a real methodological result; (b) three-seed mean±std reporting, which is above the norm in a field dominated by single-seed numbers; (c) frozen backbones with a 0.5–1.0M-parameter trainable head, which supports a defensible accuracy-per-cost claim. Lead with those.

How to read “first decision”

A 1–7 day median is not fast peer review — it is a desk-rejection statistic. At CVIU the 7-day figure sits against a 94-day decision-after-review median, meaning the median submission never reaches a referee. Treat low numbers as a warning that your title, abstract and cover letter decide the outcome, and treat 30–41 day medians (Machine Vision and Applications, Multimedia Systems) as evidence that a submission there is actually read.

6 · Ruled out, and four traps

Raise this with your advisor

Multimedia Tools and Applications is no longer an SCI journal. Clarivate removed it from the Web of Science Core Collection in the October 2024 update. Searching its ISSN on the Master Journal List today returns zero results while control searches return exact matches, and Springer's own indexing list no longer names SCIE. It carries no impact factor and no quartile.

This matters because MTAP is Prof. Yang's dominant venue — 15 of his 40 indexed journal papers, spanning 2008 to 2026, including one published after the delisting. If the lab default is to send multimedia work there, that default no longer produces an SCI paper. Signal, Image and Video Processing has already absorbed the overflow and is his active SCIE outlet.

Trap 1 — the ESCI journals that look like SCIE

Since JCR 2023, ESCI journals carry impact factors and quartiles, so metrics sites display them identically to SCIE titles. Four venues you would otherwise consider are ESCI, verified individually on the Master Journal List:

  • Journal of Imaging (MDPI) — JIF 3.8, advertised as “JCR-Q2”, but ESCI. Painful, because it is the single best topical match found: it published two CLIP-based weakly supervised VAD papers on your exact two datasets.
  • Big Data and Cognitive Computing (MDPI) — JIF 5.3, advertised “JCR-Q1”, still ESCI. The starkest case.
  • Information (MDPI) — JIF 4.3, “JCR-Q2”, ESCI.
  • Frontiers in Computer Science — JIF 3.4, ESCI.

MDPI's own pages give the game away if you read closely: they write “SCIE (Web of Science)” for Sensors, Applied Sciences and Electronics, and “ESCI (Web of Science)” for the four above. Confirm with your department whether its rule is “SCIE” or “JCR-indexed with an impact factor” — the answer decides whether Journal of Imaging is eligible.

Trap 2 — the best-category headline

Aggregators print a multi-category journal's best quartile. Image and Vision Computing is widely listed as Q1; it is Q1 in Software Engineering, Theory & Methods and Optics, and Q2 in Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence — the category anyone assessing a vision paper will actually look up, where it has been Q2 every year since 2013. The same trap applies to JVCIR, Multimedia Systems and PeerJ Computer Science, all of which are Q2 in one category and Q3 in another.

8 · What the manuscript needs first

The same upgrades keep appearing across independent venue assessments. Ranked by return per unit of GPU time, not by effort.

  1. Matched-protocol baseline re-runs. Re-run Sultani-MIL and RTFM inside your own harness — same splits, same snippet count, same three seeds, same 64-frame exclusion rule — and report them beside your 82.5%. This converts the fatal framing “82.5 versus 88–91 reported” into the survivable and more honest “under identical protocol, ours versus their reproduction.” Your existing XD sweep and RTFM repro-gap work is the seed. Nothing else moves the odds this much, and it serves every venue simultaneously.
  2. An external baseline under corruption. Right now +1.2pp mean / +13.2pp max is your method against your own model on your own benchmark. Run one reimplemented external baseline over the identical corrupted feature caches. If reliability reweighting also lifts a baseline it is a method; if it lifts only yours it is a tuning artifact. Demanded at every venue assessed.
  3. Add ShanghaiTech. Cheapest way to kill the “only two datasets” objection, which was named at almost every venue. Mandatory at IET Image Processing, where every VAD paper reports three to five datasets.
  4. Report an online/causal TTA variant alongside the transductive one. Pooling reliability statistics over a whole corruption condition is attackable as a test-set statistics leak. As written, that objection is fatal at the Q1 venues and embarrassing anywhere. Quantify what changes under a streaming protocol.
  5. Accuracy-per-cost Pareto. Trainable parameters, total parameters, FLOPs per snippet, peak VRAM, training wall-clock, inference FPS — for your four backbones and for the published competitors. A scatter of UCF AUC against trainable parameters puts you on a frontier instead of at the bottom of a leaderboard. Costs no new training.
  6. Paired significance tests everywhere. You already have the right instinct with the 8/8 sign test at p=0.008 for complementarity. Extend it systematically, report effect sizes and confidence intervals rather than bare deltas, and give the full 20-condition distribution rather than mean and max — a +13.2pp maximum next to a +1.2pp mean invites a cherry-picking charge that owning the variance defuses.
  7. Mechanism for the TTA null, if you go to CVIU or The Visual Computer. “AUC moves <0.1pp” is an observation; CVIU rejects observations. Instrument the LayerNorm head: gradient magnitude reaching the affine parameters, entropy before and after adaptation, fraction of snippet scores whose rank actually flips, and a BatchNorm-equipped control proving the null is structural rather than a tuning failure. This is the single highest-value experiment for the venues that most reward your negative result.
  8. Fix the Figure 2 erratum — RoadAccidents127 is anti-aligned with ground truth; use Fighting047, as the talk deck already does.
Good news on prior publication

CGW '26 is non-archival by written policy. Its call for papers states that accepted regular papers are disclosed only by the presenter at the conference and are “not included on the website or in the conference handbook” — no proceedings, no DOI, no ACM Digital Library or IEEE Xplore record, and the same clause appears in CGW 2025. You are extending an unpublished talk, not an indexed paper, so no duplicate-publication rule is triggered and no formal new-material quota applies.

Disclose it in the cover letter anyway — Elsevier's ethics policy explicitly exempts “a published lecture or academic thesis,” which covers both the talk and your 128-page thesis. Where a journal states its own threshold (Computers & Graphics and The Visual Computer both say 30% new content), meet it regardless; it is the safe posture and you have far more than enough thesis material.

Confirmed to exist via Crossref and OpenAlex. Your differentiators are real and should be stated explicitly: it fuses RGB with skeleton, you fuse a frozen vision-language backbone with skeleton; you freeze both encoders and train 0.5–1.0M parameters; you compare four backbones under one protocol; and you add corruption robustness and test-time adaptation, which it does not.

10 · How this was built, and what stayed unverified

Twenty research agents across two passes: a discovery sweep by publisher family plus scope-evidence, Q1-reach and local-context sweeps, then an adversarial verification pass instructed to refute rather than confirm. Roughly 2.4 million tokens and 1,550 tool calls. Nine claims from the first pass were overturned by the second.

Metrics vintage
All impact factors and quartiles are 2025 JIFs from the JCR 2026 edition, released 17 June 2026. Aggregators label the same number the “2026 impact factor” — same value, different convention. The unambiguous phrasing for a committee is “JIF released June 2026, 2025 citation year.”
Quartile source
Per-category JCR ranks were read from JCR journal profiles, not from Scimago. SJR quartiles routinely differ by one level and are frequently mislabelled as JCR by aggregator sites — IET Image Processing is Q3 under JCR and Q2 under SJR, and SPIC likewise. Confirm which system NTUST uses.
Indexing
Every SCIE-versus-ESCI determination came from the Clarivate Master Journal List with all index filters active, with positive and negative controls run in the same session to rule out filter and rendering artifacts.
Acceptance rates
Publisher-stated for only three venues in the entire survey: IET Image Processing (33%), IEEE Access (20%) and PeerJ Computer Science (33%). Every other acceptance-rate figure circulating online is an aggregator estimate. None are quoted here.
Not verified
The Pattern Recognition 2026 paper's reported numbers (ScienceDirect blocked all retrieval routes). The exact date MTAP lost SCIE coverage — the status is established, the timing is not. Neural Computing and Applications' delisting mechanism, which has no announcement behind it. ETRI Journal's and PeerJ's review-speed figures, which their publishers simply do not publish. MDPI per-journal acceptance rates, which are rendered in a JavaScript widget behind bot protection.

Full screening breadth

133 distinct journals were assessed across IEEE, Elsevier, Springer, Wiley/IET/Taylor & Francis, MDPI and open-access publishers, plus regional SCIE titles from Taiwan, Korea, Japan and China. A dedicated completeness pass hunted for missed venue families and surfaced Machine Learning (Springer, Q2, free subscription route) as the one place a null result is an asset rather than a liability — noted here for completeness, though it is a genuine reach.

The venues that institutionalise negative results as an article type — TMLR, ReScience C, the NeurIPS and ICLR reproducibility tracks, Cambridge's Experimental Results — are all outside JCR and therefore ineligible under an SCI requirement.
