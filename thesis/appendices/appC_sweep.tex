\chapter{Hyperparameter Sweep Detail}
\label{app:c}

This appendix expands the hyperparameter-sensitivity study summarized in
Section~\ref{sec:sweep}. One framing rule governs everything below: the
sweep was run in the \emph{historical} 2026-05 configuration---CLIP ViT-B/16
backbone, the pre-$\lambda_2{=}0$ smoothness setting on XD-Violence, seed 42
for the grid---which predates both the backbone study and the final
configuration of Chapter~\ref{ch:04}. Every number in this appendix is
therefore internal to that historical configuration: sweep values are
comparable to the labeled sweep-era baselines quoted alongside them, and to
nothing else in this thesis. In particular, none of the values below can be
compared against the final headline results of Chapter~\ref{ch:05}.

\section{Sweep Design and Coverage}
\label{sec:appc-design}

The sweep varied the two hyperparameters with the largest expected leverage
on the MIL head---the learning rate and the top-$k$ selection count---while
holding everything else at the historical defaults. The grid was built in
staged rounds (an initial coarse grid plus three extension rounds around
promising regions), reaching 19 learning-rate values
($5\times10^{-5}$ to $2\times10^{-3}$) crossed with six top-$k$ values
($k \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9\}$); the staged union covers 99 configurations
per dataset on the $19\times6$ canvas, i.e.\ 198 single-seed training runs
in total, plus 12 three-seed confirmation runs (four winning cells $\times$
three seeds). Each run is a complete training-plus-evaluation job under the
standard protocol (Chapter~\ref{ch:04}); at under five minutes per
configuration on a single RTX~4090, the full sweep was an overnight-scale
experiment.

As a reproducibility check, the sweep re-ran its own default cell
($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-4}$, $k=3$): it reproduced the historical sweep-era
baselines exactly---71.92\% AP on XD-Violence and 82.27\% AUC on
UCF-Crime---confirming that the sweep harness and the runs that produced
those historical baselines are consistent.

The confirmations sharpen both conclusions. The XD-Violence winner holds up
at three seeds: 74.69$\pm$2.55\% AP, $+3.72$ points over the historical
sweep-era three-seed baseline, with every individual seed---77.69 (s42),
74.93 (s123), 71.47 (s2024)---at or above that baseline's mean. The seed
spread is large (std 2.55), consistent with XD-Violence's generally higher
seed sensitivity (Chapter~\ref{ch:05}), but the direction is unambiguous.
The UCF-Crime ``winners,'' by contrast, confirm at $+0.14$ and $+0.12$
points over their historical sweep-era baseline---well within one seed-std
of zero. UCF-Crime is hyperparameter-insensitive across this entire grid.

\section{Interpretation and Disposition}
\label{sec:appc-disposition}

Mechanistically, the XD-Violence ridge at high learning rate and small $k$
suggests the historical default ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-4}$) under-trained the
fusion head on XD-Violence's larger training set, and that its shorter
anomaly events reward sharper top-$k$ selection ($k \le 2$) over the
default $k=3$ averaging. The flat UCF-Crime surface implies the same head
capacity saturates on UCF-Crime regardless of reasonable optimizer
settings---a useful negative result, since it derisks the
fixed-configuration comparisons used throughout the thesis.

As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sweep}, the sweep winner was deliberately
\emph{not} folded into the final configuration: the headline experiments
keep one fixed cross-dataset hyperparameter set for comparability across
the variant--backbone grid, and the later, independently-adopted
$\lambda_2{=}0$ setting on XD-Violence recovered most of the XD-Violence
headroom through a different mechanism. Because backbone, smoothness
setting, and seeding protocol all changed after the sweep, the historical
deltas in this appendix cannot be added to, or compared against, the final
results anywhere in this thesis; they quantify sensitivity \emph{within}
the historical 2026-05 sweep configuration, and that is the only claim they
support.
