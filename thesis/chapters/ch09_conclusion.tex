\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:09}

This thesis has presented a dual-modal fusion framework for weakly supervised
violence-oriented video anomaly detection that combines frozen skeleton
dynamics from CTR-GCN with frozen vision-language features through a learned
gated mechanism trained with MIL ranking loss. A systematic study across four
visual backbones --- CLIP ViT-B/16 and three SigLIP2 variants --- on UCF-Crime
and XD-Violence, followed by a corruption-robustness and test-time-adaptation
investigation, yields direct answers to the three research questions posed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:01}. The chapter closes by outlining the follow-up tracks the
thesis infrastructure leaves ready to run.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conc-rq}

\textbf{RQ1 --- Does fusing skeleton dynamics with frozen vision-language
features add value over either stream alone?} Yes, with an honestly modest
magnitude. Skeleton features add a small but consistent complementary gain
over visual-only baselines: gated fusion meets or exceeds its visual-only
counterpart in all eight backbone$\times$dataset configurations --- strictly
positive in six, with two ties (tie-dropping sign test, $p \approx 0.03$) ---
despite the skeleton modality's substantially lower standalone performance
(68.8\% AUC on UCF-Crime). The value of the modality is only realized through
learned gating (the mechanism tested here): fixed equal-weight late fusion falls more
than 10 AP points below gated fusion on XD-Violence on every backbone,
confirming that \emph{how} the streams are combined matters as much as
\emph{whether} they are combined. The headline results are $82.5$\% frame-level
AUC on UCF-Crime (gated fusion, SigLIP2 Giant) and $78.7$\% AP on XD-Violence
(gated fusion, SigLIP2 SO400M), each reported as a three-seed mean.

\textbf{RQ2 --- How sensitive are these results to the choice of
vision-language backbone?} Backbone choice interacts with dataset
characteristics in non-trivial ways. SigLIP2 Giant achieves the best UCF-Crime
result (82.5\% AUC), while SigLIP2 SO400M leads on XD-Violence (78.7\% AP);
the visual-only XD-Violence backbones sit within seed variance of one another,
so this preference emerges through fusion rather than from visual-only
strength. These dataset-specific preferences underscore the importance of
multi-backbone evaluation for VAD system design --- a backbone ranking taken
from general-purpose benchmarks would not have predicted the fusion ranking
observed here.

\textbf{RQ3 --- Does entropy-minimization test-time adaptation transfer from
BatchNorm networks to a frozen LayerNorm-based fusion head?} No. TENT- and
SAR-style entropy minimization, restricted by the architecture to the 1{,}536
LayerNorm affine scalars of the fusion head, leaves rank-based detection
metrics essentially unchanged ($<0.1$ points across all four backbones under
the three-seed continual protocol) --- a null result we argue is structural,
within the evaluated continual protocol and this 1{,}536-parameter adaptation
surface, rather than a tuning failure: LayerNorm keeps no recalibratable
batch statistics, and affine nudges of this size cannot reorder the pooled
rankings on which AUC depends. What does
recover robustness is changing which modality the frozen fusion trusts: the
proposed label-free discriminative-reliability reweighting improves
corrupted-AUC on all four backbones (mean $+1.21$ points, up to $+13.2$ points
in the condition that most collapses the visual-language stream), concentrated
where one modality remains reliable, without updating any parameters. The gains concern
corrupted-input robustness under a transductive protocol --- not clean
benchmark performance --- and are claimed as nothing more.

\section{Modularity and Efficiency}
\label{sec:conc-modularity}

The framework provides a modular, computationally efficient approach to
multi-modal VAD that requires training only the lightweight fusion head ---
0.50M to 1.02M trainable parameters depending on backbone --- while leveraging
frozen pre-trained encoders. Training completes in under 5 minutes per
configuration on a single RTX 4090, which is what made the full study
feasible: four backbones, six fusion variants, two datasets, and three seeds
per configuration, plus the entire corruption and adaptation grid, all on one
consumer GPU. The frozen-backbone design also future-proofs the pipeline:
evaluating a newly released vision-language model requires one feature
extraction pass and minutes of head training, without retraining anything
else. We do not claim state-of-the-art benchmark performance --- Chapter
\ref{ch:07} places the results honestly in a field whose strongest systems
reach the high-80s to low-90s on UCF-Crime under heavier budgets --- but
within the frozen, visual-plus-skeleton, single-GPU regime, the pipeline is a
competitive and unusually reproducible operating point.

\section{Code and Data Availability}
\label{sec:conc-code}

The codebase and experiment configurations will be released to support
reproducibility and facilitate future multi-backbone VAD studies. All headline
numbers are three-seed means on fixed splits under a single evaluation
protocol, and the UCF-Crime headline is reproducible from a committed
frame-count manifest. The released repository provides the complete
reproduction path, from environment setup through feature extraction,
training, evaluation, and figure generation.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conc-future}

The thesis infrastructure --- cached features, tracked run index, fixed
splits, and a sub-five-minute training loop --- leaves several follow-up
tracks ready to run. We list them as forward pointers only; none of the
numbers below are results of this thesis.

\textbf{Temporal modeling over snippets.} The fusion head scores each snippet
independently; the analysis in Chapter~\ref{ch:07} attributes the residual gap
to CLIP-TSA largely to its temporal self-attention machinery. A lightweight
temporal module (a TCN or single transformer layer over the snippet sequence)
is the largest single untapped headroom, but it changes the headline
architecture and would cascade through every table, so it was deliberately
deferred to a thesis extension rather than bolted on late.

\textbf{XD-Violence test-score smoothing (verified, deliberately not
adopted).} A simple post-hoc temporal smoothing of XD-Violence test scores
over a 64-frame window --- matching the native snippet length --- was verified
to add $+0.57$ points AP ($78.72 \to 79.29$, consistent across all three
seeds).\footnote{Tracked in the repository review artifact
\texttt{.planning/REVIEW-2026-06-10.md}, Section~5 (Pri-3), together with the
window-plateau analysis.} It is \emph{not} incorporated into any number in
this thesis: it was identified after the result freeze, and adopting a
post-hoc protocol change that moves the headline would undercut the
reproducibility discipline the thesis argues for. The follow-up is to
pre-register the smoothing window (showing the flat plateau around $w=64$
preempts the tuning objection) and adopt it in a future revision of the
pipeline.

\textbf{Skeleton-head ensemble routing.} The current reweighting routes
\emph{within} the frozen gated-fusion head. Routing instead to a separately
trained skeleton-only head (a reliability-routed score-level ensemble) is
estimated to add another 1--2 points to the corrupted-AUC mean, because the
skeleton stream's clean cache remains intact under the corruption families
where the fused head bottoms out; it would also likely convert the single-seed
SigLIP2-Base over-routing disclosure into a positive result.

\textbf{Streaming reliability estimation.} The routed signal is two scalars
per condition, which should converge quickly over a stream of test snippets. A
causal, streaming estimate of the routing weight would discharge the
transductive limitation (Section~\ref{sec:lim-tta}) and upgrade the claim class from transductive to
online; until that experiment is run, no online claim is made.

\textbf{JPEG-family rescue diagnostic.} The reweighting is exactly zero on the
JPEG family by gate construction, because both streams degrade there --- the
largest untouched pool in the corruption study. Corrupted skeleton caches for
this family already exist, so a skeleton-only diagnostic would settle, with a
pre-registered kill criterion, whether any routing-based mechanism can help
where the skeleton stream itself is damaged.

\textbf{Text-prompt alignment branch.} The vision-language text tower is
entirely unused in this thesis. A lightweight VadCLIP-style alignment branch
over the cached joint-space features is the natural way to test whether the
text modality's gains survive the frozen-backbone constraint, and is the
flagship candidate for a follow-on extension.

\textbf{Adapter-based test-time adaptation.} Given the LayerNorm barrier
identified in Chapter~\ref{ch:07}, adaptation mechanisms that alter the
features themselves --- LoRA-style low-rank adapters or visual prompt tuning
--- are the principled next step for test-time robustness, as they sidestep
the affine-parameter bottleneck entirely.

\textbf{Audio for XD-Violence.} XD-Violence provides audio annotations that
our visual-skeleton pipeline does not exploit; audio cues are highly
discriminative for categories such as Shooting and Explosion, and the fusion
architecture extends naturally to a third stream.

\textbf{Additional backbones and datasets.} The frozen design makes new
vision-language backbones cheap to evaluate as they are released, and
extending the study to ShanghaiTech or CUHK Avenue would test how much of the
skeleton-complementarity finding is specific to violence-oriented anomalies.

\bigskip

Together, the findings of this thesis demonstrate that the combination of skeleton dynamics
and visual-language semantics, coupled with careful backbone selection and
honest evaluation of adaptation techniques, advances our understanding of
practical weakly supervised violence detection systems --- and that a
reproducible result a reader can trust, obtained under transparent
constraints, is a contribution worth stating plainly.
