\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:09}

This thesis has presented a dual-modal fusion framework for weakly supervised
violence-oriented video anomaly detection that combines frozen skeleton
dynamics from CTR-GCN with frozen vision-language features through a learned
gated mechanism trained with MIL ranking loss. A systematic study across four
visual backbones --- CLIP ViT-B/16 and three SigLIP2 variants --- on UCF-Crime
and XD-Violence, followed by a corruption-robustness and test-time-adaptation
investigation, yields direct answers to the three research questions posed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:01}.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conc-rq}

\textbf{RQ1 --- Does fusing skeleton dynamics with frozen vision-language
features add value over either stream alone?} Yes, with an honestly modest
magnitude. Skeleton features add a small but consistent complementary gain
over visual-only baselines: gated fusion meets or exceeds its visual-only
counterpart in all eight backbone$\times$dataset configurations --- strictly
positive in six, with two ties (tie-dropping sign test, $p \approx 0.03$) ---
despite the skeleton modality's substantially lower standalone performance
(68.8\% AUC on UCF-Crime). The value of the modality is only realized through
learned, input-dependent gating: fixed equal-weight late fusion falls more
than 10 AP points below gated fusion on XD-Violence on every backbone,
confirming that \emph{how} the streams are combined matters as much as
\emph{whether} they are combined. The headline results are $82.5$\% frame-level
AUC on UCF-Crime (gated fusion, SigLIP2 Giant) and $78.7$\% AP on XD-Violence
(gated fusion, SigLIP2 SO400M), each reported as a three-seed mean.

\textbf{RQ2 --- How sensitive are these results to the choice of
vision-language backbone?} Backbone choice interacts with dataset
characteristics in non-trivial ways. SigLIP2 Giant achieves the best UCF-Crime
result (82.5\% AUC), while SigLIP2 SO400M leads on XD-Violence (78.7\% AP);
the visual-only XD-Violence backbones sit within seed variance of one another,
so this preference emerges through fusion rather than from visual-only
strength. These dataset-specific preferences underscore the importance of
multi-backbone evaluation for VAD system design --- a backbone ranking taken
from general-purpose benchmarks would not have predicted the fusion ranking
observed here.

\textbf{RQ3 --- Does entropy-minimization test-time adaptation transfer from
BatchNorm networks to a frozen LayerNorm-based fusion head?} No. TENT- and
SAR-style entropy minimization, restricted by the architecture to the 1{,}536
LayerNorm affine scalars of the fusion head, leaves rank-based detection
metrics essentially unchanged ($<0.1$ points across all four backbones under
the three-seed continual protocol) --- a structural consequence of LayerNorm's
lack of recalibratable batch statistics, not a tuning failure. What does
recover robustness is changing which modality the frozen fusion trusts: the
proposed label-free discriminative-reliability reweighting improves
corrupted-AUC on all four backbones (mean $+1.21$ points, up to $+13.2$ points
in the condition that most collapses the visual-language stream), concentrated
where one modality remains reliable, without updating any parameters. The gains concern
corrupted-input robustness under a transductive protocol --- not clean
benchmark performance --- and are claimed as nothing more.

\section{Modularity and Efficiency}
\label{sec:conc-modularity}

The framework provides a modular, computationally efficient approach to
multi-modal VAD that requires training only the lightweight fusion head ---
0.50M to 1.02M trainable parameters depending on backbone --- while leveraging
frozen pre-trained encoders. Training completes in under 5 minutes per
configuration on a single RTX 4090, which is what made the full study
feasible: four backbones, six fusion variants, two datasets, and three seeds
per configuration, plus the entire corruption and adaptation grid, all on one
consumer GPU. The frozen-backbone design also future-proofs the pipeline:
evaluating a newly released vision-language model requires one feature
extraction pass and minutes of head training, without retraining anything
else. We do not claim state-of-the-art benchmark performance --- Chapter
\ref{ch:07} places the results honestly in a field whose strongest systems
reach the high-80s to low-90s on UCF-Crime under heavier budgets --- but
within the frozen, visual-plus-skeleton, single-GPU regime, the pipeline is a
competitive and unusually reproducible operating point.

\section{Code and Data Availability}
\label{sec:conc-code}

The codebase and experiment configurations will be released to support
reproducibility and facilitate future multi-backbone VAD studies. All headline
numbers are three-seed means on fixed splits under a single evaluation
protocol, and the UCF-Crime headline is reproducible from a committed
frame-count manifest; Appendix~\ref{app:e} provides the complete
reproduction walkthrough, from environment setup through feature extraction,
training, evaluation, and figure generation.

\bigskip

Together, these findings demonstrate that the combination of skeleton dynamics
and visual-language semantics, coupled with careful backbone selection and
honest evaluation of adaptation techniques, advances our understanding of
practical weakly supervised violence detection systems --- and that a
reproducible result a reader can trust, obtained under transparent
constraints, is a contribution worth stating plainly.
