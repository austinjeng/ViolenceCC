\chapter{Related Work}
\label{ch:02}

This thesis draws on four research lineages: weakly supervised video anomaly detection
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-wsvad}), skeleton-based action recognition and its use in anomaly detection
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-skeleton}), vision-language backbones as frozen feature extractors
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-vlm}), and test-time adaptation under distribution shift
(Section~\ref{sec:rw-tta}). Each section traces the developments most relevant to the design
decisions of Chapter~\ref{ch:03} and closes by positioning this work within the lineage.
Quantitative comparisons against the methods surveyed here are deferred to the verified
comparison tables of Chapter~\ref{ch:07}; this chapter describes mechanisms and lineage, not
leaderboards.

\section{Weakly Supervised Video Anomaly Detection}
\label{sec:rw-wsvad}

\subsection{MIL Ranking Foundations}

The weakly supervised formulation of video anomaly detection was established by Sultani et
al.~\cite{sultani2018ucfcrime}, who made two lasting contributions. The first is the UCF-Crime
dataset: 1,900 untrimmed real-world surveillance videos across thirteen anomaly categories, with
only video-level labels for training and frame-level annotations reserved for evaluation. The
second is the multiple instance learning (MIL) ranking framework that nearly all subsequent work
builds on: each video is a bag of temporal segments, and a ranking loss drives the maximum
predicted anomaly score of an anomalous bag above that of a normal bag, supplemented by sparsity
and temporal-smoothness regularizers. With C3D features, the original method reported 75.41\%
frame-level ROC-AUC on UCF-Crime, the reference point from which the field has advanced.

Wu et al.~\cite{wu2020xdviolence} broadened the empirical foundation with XD-Violence, a
4,754-video multi-modal benchmark whose footage comes from movies, sports broadcasts, news, and
online media rather than fixed surveillance cameras, and which is evaluated by frame-level average
precision (AP) rather than ROC-AUC. Their HL-Net baseline also introduced audio as an auxiliary
modality, foreshadowing the multi-modal directions surveyed below. Together, UCF-Crime and
XD-Violence remain the two principal violence-oriented WSVAD benchmarks and are the two used
throughout this thesis.

\subsection{Feature-Magnitude, Memory, and Uncertainty Methods}

A second generation of methods refined how the MIL signal is extracted from precomputed motion
features. RTFM~\cite{tian2021rtfm} (Robust Temporal Feature Magnitude learning) observed that
selecting instances by raw anomaly score is brittle and instead learns feature magnitudes such
that the top-$k$ largest-magnitude segments discriminate anomalous from normal bags, combined
with dilated temporal convolutions and self-attention; with I3D features it reached 84.30\% AUC on
UCF-Crime and 77.81\% AP on XD-Violence, and its top-$k$ selection and regularization scheme have
become standard components---including in the training objective adopted in
Chapter~\ref{ch:03} of this thesis.

UR-DMU~\cite{zhou2023urdmu} augments the MIL pipeline with dual memory units---separate learnable
memory banks for normal and abnormal prototypes---together with an uncertainty-regulated
representation that models normal-pattern variance explicitly, allowing the detector to separate
hard normal segments from genuinely anomalous ones. MGFN~\cite{chen2023mgfn} (Magnitude-Contrastive
Glance-and-Focus Network) mimics human inspection by first \emph{glancing} over the whole video to
acquire global context and then \emph{focusing} on candidate anomalous regions, with a
magnitude-contrastive loss that encourages separability of normal and abnormal feature magnitudes
across scenes. Both lines demonstrate that architectural priors on \emph{how} evidence is
accumulated over time can substantially improve a fixed feature representation---a lesson that
transfers to the fusion-head design in this thesis, which likewise keeps its features frozen and
concentrates learning in a small evidence-aggregation module.

Efficiency-oriented variants also exist within this generation:
Light-WVAD~\cite{wang2024lightwvad} combines adaptive instance selection with a deliberately
lightweight architecture to cut parameter count while remaining competitive on UCF-Crime,
a design goal shared by the sub-million-parameter fusion head of this thesis.

\subsection{The CLIP Era}

The arrival of strong vision-language models shifted the field's feature basis from
action-recognition backbones (C3D, I3D) to contrastively trained image encoders.
CLIP-TSA~\cite{joo2023cliptsa} was among the first to pair frozen CLIP features with a temporal
self-attention module for WSVAD, showing that image-text pretraining transfers to anomaly
detection despite CLIP never having seen anomaly labels. VadCLIP~\cite{wu2024vadclip} went
further, exploiting not only CLIP's visual features but its aligned text space: a dual-branch
design combines a conventional binary scoring branch with a language-image alignment branch driven
by learnable prompts, reaching 88.02\% AUC on UCF-Crime and 84.51\% AP on XD-Violence and
demonstrating how much headroom text alignment adds over purely visual scoring.

Several works refined the text-side machinery. TPWNG~\cite{yang2024tpwng} uses text prompts with
normality guidance to generate pseudo-labels for finer temporal alignment.
PEL4VAD~\cite{pu2024pel4vad} enhances context features through prompt-based semantic priors drawn
from external knowledge, tightening the alignment between anomaly concepts and visual context.
STPrompt~\cite{wu2024stprompt} learns spatio-temporal prompt embeddings to localize anomalies in
both time and space while suppressing background influence. TEVAD~\cite{chen2023tevad} takes a
complementary route, augmenting visual features with text features derived from automatically
generated video captions to capture semantics that appearance alone misses.
AnomalyCLIP~\cite{zanella2024anomalyclip} analyses CLIP's latent space directly, isolating a
normality subspace and scoring anomalies by directed distances in that space, and additionally
addresses anomaly \emph{recognition} (classifying the anomaly type) alongside detection.

Two aspects of this lineage matter for the present thesis. First, all of these methods are
single-modality in the sense that every input signal ultimately derives from the RGB frames
consumed by one visual backbone (plus, in some cases, its paired text encoder); none incorporates
an explicit articulated-motion modality. Second, the strongest results in this family rely on
text-branch supervision or backbone adaptation, whereas the pipeline studied here deliberately
keeps the backbone frozen and uses no text branch---a design choice whose cost and benefits are
quantified in Chapters~\ref{ch:05} and~\ref{ch:07}.

\subsection{MLLM-Based and Training-Free Methods}

The most recent shift leverages large language models and multi-modal LLMs.
LAVAD~\cite{zanella2024lavad} is entirely training-free: off-the-shelf captioning models describe
each frame, and an LLM aggregates and scores the resulting textual descriptions, producing
anomaly estimates with no VAD-specific training at all. EventVAD~\cite{shao2025eventvad} likewise
avoids task-specific training, first segmenting videos into events via dynamic graph analysis and
then scoring events with a multi-modal LLM. Holmes-VAD~\cite{zhang2024holmesvad} and its
successor Holmes-VAU~\cite{zhang2025holmesvau} fine-tune multi-modal LLMs on purpose-built
instruction datasets to produce not only anomaly scores but textual explanations, moving toward
anomaly \emph{understanding} at multiple temporal granularities. These systems demonstrate
impressive flexibility, but their inference cost is orders of magnitude above a lightweight
fusion head, and their scores are produced by generative reasoning rather than a calibrated
ranking head---trade-offs discussed further in Chapter~\ref{ch:07}.

\subsection{Recent Multi-Modal and Structured Directions}

Current work increasingly enriches WSVAD with additional modalities or structured representations.
PI-VAD~\cite{majhi2025pivad} augments RGB representations with five auxiliary modalities---including
pose, depth, and optical flow---distilled into the backbone via a poly-modal inductor, notably
using auxiliary modalities only at training time. DSANet~\cite{yin2026dsanet} disentangles
scene-general from scene-specific semantics to tell apart visually similar events across contexts.
GS-MoE~\cite{damicantonio2025gsmoe} routes features through a mixture of experts guided by Gaussian
splatter representations, capturing fine-grained temporal structure. PiercingEye~\cite{leng2026piercingeye}
learns in a dual Euclidean--hyperbolic space to model the hierarchical severity structure of
violent events. On the audio-visual side, HyperVD~\cite{peng2024hypervd} embeds audio-visual
features in hyperbolic space for violence detection on XD-Violence, and Ghadiya et
al.~\cite{ghadiya2024crossmodal} fuse audio and visual streams with cross-modal attention,
both exploiting XD-Violence's audio track, which purely visual methods (including ours) do not use.

The pose-as-auxiliary-signal element of PI-VAD~\cite{majhi2025pivad} is the closest point of
contact with this thesis, and the contrast is instructive: PI-VAD distills pose (among other
modalities) into a single fused representation inside a trained backbone pipeline, whereas this
thesis keeps the skeleton stream as an explicit, separately encoded modality entering a
lightweight learned gate---which is precisely what enables the test-time modality routing of
Chapter~\ref{ch:06}, an option unavailable when auxiliary modalities are absorbed at training
time. To our knowledge, no prior WSVAD system combines a frozen skeleton-graph encoder with
frozen vision-language features through a learned gate, nor evaluates such a system across
multiple visual backbones.

\subsection{Design-Space Synthesis}

Table~\ref{tab:rw-taxonomy} summarizes the surveyed WSVAD lineage along the mechanism axes that
matter for this thesis: the feature basis each method scores, any additional signal beyond a
single visual stream, whether a text branch participates, and the nature of task-specific
training. Three observations emerge. First, the field's feature basis has migrated twice---from
action-recognition backbones (C3D, I3D) to frozen vision-language encoders, and most recently to
multi-modal LLMs---while the weak-supervision protocol has remained essentially constant, which
is why MIL ranking machinery from the first generation still underlies current systems. Second,
where additional signals appear, they are predominantly \emph{textual} (prompts, captions,
alignment branches) or \emph{auditory}; explicit articulated-motion signals are nearly absent,
appearing only as one of several distilled training-time auxiliaries in
PI-VAD~\cite{majhi2025pivad}. Third, no surveyed method keeps an auxiliary modality as a
separately encoded stream behind a learned gate at \emph{inference} time---the structural
property this thesis introduces and later exploits for test-time robustness
(Chapter~\ref{ch:06}).

\section{Skeleton-Based Action Recognition and Anomaly Detection}
\label{sec:rw-skeleton}

Skeleton-based action recognition classifies actions from sequences of human body keypoints
rather than raw pixels. The modern lineage begins with ST-GCN~\cite{yan2018stgcn}, which
represents the skeleton sequence as a spatio-temporal graph---joints as nodes, bones and temporal
continuity as edges---and applies graph convolutions over this fixed, anatomically defined
topology. Subsequent work progressively relaxed the fixed topology; CTR-GCN~\cite{chen2021ctrgcn}
represents the state of this progression, learning \emph{channel-wise topology refinement}: a
shared base topology is refined per feature channel, so different channels can attend to
different joint relationships (for example, hand--hand interactions for clapping versus
hip--knee--ankle chains for kicking). CTR-GCN's strong performance on the NTU RGB+D benchmarks,
together with the reproducible training recipes and pretrained checkpoints of the PYSKL
toolbox~\cite{yan2022pyskl}, make it a natural frozen encoder choice; this thesis uses the PYSKL
NTU RGB+D 120 checkpoint with all four input streams (joint, bone, joint motion, bone motion) as
described in Chapter~\ref{ch:03}.

Skeleton input must itself be extracted from video. This thesis uses
RTMPose~\cite{jiang2023rtmpose}, a real-time top-down pose estimator: a person detector proposes
boxes, and a lightweight pose network regresses seventeen COCO-format keypoints per person via
SimCC-style coordinate classification, providing an accuracy--throughput operating point suitable
for processing hundreds of hours of surveillance footage on a single GPU.

In anomaly detection, skeleton representations have been explored primarily in single-modal
settings. Doshi and Yilmaz~\cite{doshi2022skeleton} formulate anomaly detection over pose
trajectories in a modular framework, arguing that pose-based signals are robust to appearance
nuisances and privacy-preserving relative to pixel-based methods. Skeleton-only anomaly detection
performs best in constrained scenes (fixed cameras, pedestrians at moderate scale) where pose
estimation is reliable and anomalies are motion-defined. Its known weaknesses---missed detections
at low resolution, crowded scenes, and non-human anomalies such as explosions or vehicle
accidents---are precisely the cases where appearance-based features excel, motivating the fusion
studied here rather than a choice between the two modalities. Classical multi-stream fusion in
action recognition, such as two-stream I3D over RGB and optical flow~\cite{carreira2017i3d},
established that complementary input encodings improve recognition; this thesis transposes that
principle to the weakly supervised anomaly setting with skeleton and vision-language streams.

One further property of the skeleton modality matters specifically for the robustness study of
this thesis. Once extracted, pose keypoints are low-dimensional geometric quantities: pixel-domain
corruptions can affect them only \emph{through} the pose estimator, and when skeleton features are
computed from clean frames---or served from a cache extracted before corruption occurs---they are
entirely untouched by degradation of the visual stream. The two modalities therefore fail under
\emph{different} conditions: additive noise devastates dense visual embeddings while leaving a
cached skeleton stream intact, whereas motion blur or aggressive compression can corrupt pose
estimation itself. This asymmetric failure profile, largely incidental in prior skeleton-based
anomaly work, becomes a structural asset in Chapter~\ref{ch:06}, where the fusion is routed toward
whichever stream remains reliable under the deployed corruption.

\section{Vision-Language Backbones}
\label{sec:rw-vlm}

The visual features in this thesis come from frozen vision-language image encoders, all built on
the Vision Transformer~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit}, which processes an image as a sequence of
patches with self-attention. CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} trains such an encoder jointly with a
text encoder by contrastive learning over 400 million web image--text pairs, producing
representations whose semantic breadth transfers to downstream tasks without fine-tuning---the
property WSVAD methods of the CLIP era exploit. SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} replaces CLIP's
softmax-based contrastive objective with a pairwise sigmoid loss, which removes the need for a
global normalization over the batch and improves training efficiency and small-batch behavior.
SigLIP2~\cite{tschannen2025siglip2} extends this recipe into a multilingual family trained with
additional objectives---captioning-based pretraining, self-supervised distillation, and masked
prediction---yielding improved dense and localization features across a range of model scales,
including a shape-optimized ${\sim}$400M-parameter encoder (SO400M) whose width/depth allocation
follows compute-optimal scaling analysis, and a giant-scale variant.

Two practical properties of frozen vision-language features shape how they are used in WSVAD
pipelines, including ours. First, frozen features are \emph{deterministic}: extracted once and
cached, they make every downstream experiment cheap and exactly repeatable, which is what makes
large ablation grids and multi-seed protocols affordable on a single GPU. Second, freezing
decouples the representation from the task head, so comparisons between heads (or, in this
thesis, between fusion mechanisms and backbones) are not confounded by divergent fine-tuning of
the encoder. The cost is a ceiling: task-adapted or text-aligned systems consistently outperform
frozen-feature ones, because the frozen representation cannot reshape itself around the anomaly
signal. This thesis accepts that ceiling deliberately and quantifies it honestly
(Chapters~\ref{ch:05} and~\ref{ch:07}). A further design choice within the frozen regime is
whether to use the backbone's paired \emph{text} encoder: most CLIP-era WSVAD methods above do,
through prompts or alignment branches, while this thesis uses only the image tower---keeping the
pipeline free of prompt engineering and its associated tuning surface, at a measurable cost in
headline accuracy.

This thesis treats the backbone as an experimental variable rather than a fixed choice: the same
pipeline is evaluated over CLIP ViT-B/16, SigLIP2 ViT-B/16, SigLIP2 SO400M, and SigLIP2 Giant
(exact checkpoints in Chapter~\ref{ch:03}), spanning two training objectives and an order of
magnitude in encoder capacity. Prior WSVAD work almost universally reports a single backbone;
the cross-backbone and cross-dataset interactions this design exposes are reported in
Chapter~\ref{ch:05}.

\section{Test-Time Adaptation}
\label{sec:rw-tta}

Test-time adaptation (TTA) adjusts a deployed model using only unlabeled test data, aiming to
recover accuracy lost to distribution shift---sensor noise, blur, compression, weather---without
retraining on labeled source data. The corruption-robustness setting that frames most TTA
research was standardized by ImageNet-C~\cite{hendrycks2019imagenetc}, which applies synthetic
corruptions at graded severities to a clean test set; the UCF-Crime-C benchmark constructed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:04} follows this parameterization style.

Two families of TTA methods are relevant here. The first adapts \emph{normalization statistics}.
Schneider et al.~\cite{schneider2020norm} showed that much of the corruption-induced accuracy
loss in BatchNorm~\cite{ioffe2015batchnorm} networks can be removed simply by replacing the
running activation statistics estimated on clean training data with statistics computed on the
corrupted test batch---no gradients required. This works because BatchNorm layers store an
explicit summary of the source distribution that becomes stale under shift. Relatedly,
CORAL~\cite{sun2016coral} aligns the second-order statistics (covariance) of source and target
features through a linear map---a classic, hyperparameter-light domain adaptation technique that
can be applied at test time to whiten-and-recolor corrupted features toward clean statistics.

The second family performs \emph{entropy minimization}. TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} adapts the
affine parameters of normalization layers by minimizing the Shannon entropy of the model's
predictions on each test batch, sharpening decision confidence under shift; it established
fully test-time adaptation as a practical setting. SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} hardens this recipe for
deployment conditions in which plain entropy minimization collapses---small batches, class-imbalanced
streams, mixed shifts---by filtering out high-entropy (unreliable) samples from the update and
seeking flat minima via sharpness-aware minimization~\cite{foret2021sam}.

Beyond the choice of objective, TTA methods differ in \emph{protocol}, and the distinction is
consequential. Episodic protocols reset the adapted parameters after each test unit (each video,
in our setting), bounding error accumulation at the cost of adaptation capacity; continual-online
protocols let adaptation accumulate along the test stream, which is the setting TENT and SAR were
designed for but which exposes them to drift and collapse on long, imbalanced streams---the
failure mode SAR's filtering explicitly targets. A second, less-discussed interaction is with the
\emph{evaluation metric}: ranking metrics such as AUC are invariant to any strictly monotone
transformation of the scores, so an adaptation step changes the metric only insofar as it
\emph{reorders} test samples. Both distinctions---protocol validity and metric invariance---turn
out to be central to the TTA analysis of this thesis (Section~\ref{sec:meth-tta} and
Chapter~\ref{ch:06}).

Both families were designed around BatchNorm. LayerNorm~\cite{ba2016layernorm}, which normalizes
each sample independently and maintains \emph{no} running statistics, changes the picture
structurally: there is no stale source summary to refresh, so statistic-replacement methods have
no direct target, and entropy-based methods can only nudge the LayerNorm affine parameters. SAR
itself reports results on LayerNorm-based vision transformers, where the affine parameters remain
the adaptation surface. What happens when that surface is confined to a small \emph{frozen-model
fusion head}---and the evaluation metric is a rank statistic (AUC) invariant to monotone score
changes---has not been examined in the VAD literature. Chapter~\ref{ch:06} shows empirically that
in this configuration entropy minimization leaves the metric essentially unchanged on every
backbone, that CORAL/NORM-style feature restoration is rank-disruptive with a backbone-dependent
sign, and that a label-free reweighting of the two modality streams---exploiting the dual-modal
structure that single-stream TTA methods do not have---recovers corrupted-input accuracy where
those baselines cannot.
