\chapter{Results: Robustness and Test-Time Adaptation}
\label{ch:06}

This chapter reports the robustness study on UCF-Crime-C
(Chapter~\ref{ch:04}) and the test-time adaptation investigation it anchors.
The chapter tells the TTA story in full, including its negative results,
because the path matters: entropy-minimization TTA is shown to be structurally
inert for this architecture (Section~\ref{sec:entropy-tta}),
feature-statistic restoration is shown to be rank-disruptive
(Section~\ref{sec:coral}), and the method that does work---the
discriminative-reliability reweighting of
Chapter~\ref{ch:03}---succeeds precisely by abandoning parameter and feature
adaptation in favor of routing between modalities
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-results}). Section~\ref{sec:ln-barrier} closes with the
mechanism analysis. Throughout, reported gains are corrupted-AUC robustness
gains, stated in points; no method in this chapter changes clean-data
accuracy, and none is claimed to.

A protocol note applies to every number in this chapter: corrupted-condition
AUCs are computed on the truncated snippet-aligned frame grid (a whole number
of 64-frame windows, Chapter~\ref{ch:04}), so they are internally consistent
with one another but not directly comparable in absolute terms to the
full-length frame-grid numbers of Chapter~\ref{ch:05}.

We first establish how much corruption costs the unadapted models.
Figure~\ref{fig:corruption-source} maps source-only AUC over all 20
conditions. Averaged over the 20 conditions, the four backbones fall to
63.7 (CLIP ViT-B/16), 57.0 (SigLIP2 Base), 58.9 (SigLIP2 SO400M), and 62.8
(SigLIP2 Giant) AUC---roughly twenty points below the clean full-length-grid
headline of 82.5\% (Chapter~\ref{ch:05}), with the protocol caveat above in
mind. Three observations shape everything that follows. First, the damage is
large: corruption at ImageNet-C severities is not a marginal perturbation for
a frozen VLM stream but a collapse of much of its discriminative signal.
Second, the damage is uneven across backbones: SigLIP2 Base loses the most
and CLIP the least, and the backbone ranking under corruption does not match
the clean ranking---robustness is its own axis. Third, the damage is uneven
across families: Gaussian noise at high severities is the
deepest failure mode for the visual-language stream (in absolute fused
AUC several JPEG cells sit lower still;
Section~\ref{sec:disc-results}), while brightness
shift leaves the stream comparatively intact. This
unevenness---which stream is damaged, and when---is exactly the structure the
reweighting method of Section~\ref{sec:disc-results} exploits.

\section{Entropy-Minimization TTA: TENT and SAR}
\label{sec:entropy-tta}

\subsection{Adaptation protocol}

TENT~\cite{wang2021tent} and SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} adapt normalization-layer
parameters by minimizing prediction entropy on unlabeled test batches; SAR
adds sharpness-aware minimization~\cite{foret2021sam} and entropy-based
sample filtering. Applied to our architecture, they adapt only the LayerNorm
affine parameters ($\gamma,\beta$) of the gated fusion head---three LN layers
totaling 1{,}536 parameters (six tensors)---since the backbones, gate, and
MIL head are frozen. The final protocol is deterministic and
continual-online: parameters are reset once per corruption stream, then
adapted across the whole per-condition stream (TENT: $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$;
SAR: $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$, $\rho=0.05$), with all results reported as means
over the three training seeds \{42, 123, 2024\}. Arriving at this protocol
required two corrections that we document as methods lessons.

\subsection{A cautionary note: dropout in adaptation mode}

The first exploratory TTA runs reported a small TENT/SAR improvement. A code
audit found the cause: configuring the model for adaptation had set the
fusion head to training mode, leaving its dropout layers \emph{active at test
time}. The resulting stochastic scores carried roughly 1.07 points of
per-condition AUC noise (0.24 points on the 20-condition mean)---enough to
manufacture an apparent gain out of luck. After disabling dropout during
adaptation and seeding all remaining randomness, repeated runs became
bit-identical and the apparent improvement vanished.\footnote{The fix is
commit \texttt{83b8927} in the project repository; every TTA number in this
thesis postdates it.} We flag this because the failure mode is generic: any
TTA evaluation that toggles \texttt{train()} mode on a module containing
dropout can mistake evaluation stochasticity for adaptation gains, and
rank-based metrics such as AUC are especially good at hiding the mechanism.

\subsection{The episodic grid and its misleading bests}

% Source: results/results-index.csv (tracked; 680 TTA rows total -- 80
% source_only / 140 tent / 460 sar -- covering the 500-run episodic grid and
% subsequent protocol reruns). Best-condition deltas are per-condition BESTS
% from the episodic grid, not means (PROVENANCE row 13).
The initial evaluation was a 500-run episodic grid over all 20 conditions:
20 source-only runs, 80 TENT runs (learning rates
$\{10^{-4}, 5\times10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 5\times10^{-3}\}$), and 400 SAR runs
(the same learning rates crossed with
$\rho \in \{0.001, 0.005, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1\}$), under a per-video adaptation
protocol that reset parameters between videos. (The master results index
records 680 TTA rows in total, including the subsequent continual-protocol
reruns.) Read naively, the grid contains encouraging cells: the best TENT
configuration improved one condition by $+3.81$ points (JPEG severity 2) and
the best SAR configuration improved another by $+5.38$ points (Gaussian
severity 5). We report these as what they are---per-condition \emph{bests}
selected post hoc across a 24-configuration hyperparameter grid---and not as
evidence of adaptation benefit: the corresponding grid-wide means were near
zero, and selecting the best cell per condition after seeing results is
exactly the practice the reproducibility protocol of Chapter~\ref{ch:04}
exists to prevent.

\subsection{Episodic inertness and the continual protocol}

The deterministic re-runs after the dropout fix produced a suspicious
pattern: exactly $+0.000$ points everywhere. The explanation is structural:
98.4\% of UCF-Crime test videos contain at most 32 snippets and therefore fit
in a single adaptation batch. Under the episodic per-video protocol---adapt
on a batch, score, then reset---the adapted parameters never influenced a
scored output for those videos: each video was scored by parameters adapted
on at most its own single chunk, then discarded. The protocol was
structurally incapable of showing an effect, in either direction.

We therefore switched to TENT and SAR's native continual-online protocol
(one reset per corruption stream, adaptation accumulating across the
condition), and verified with a sanity probe that adaptation now measurably
changes scored outputs. Every entropy-minimization number reported below is
from this corrected protocol. The lesson generalizes: episodic TTA protocols
borrowed from image classification can silently degenerate on video
benchmarks, where the natural episode (a video) is often no larger than a
single adaptation batch.

\section{Feature-Statistic Restoration and the Strategy Search}
\label{sec:coral}

% Source (narrative + exploration numbers): .knowledge/tta-to-reweighting.html
% (tracked post-mortem, compiled 2026-06-11); underlying per-condition data
% results/_coral_derisk/ (tracked r1full/variants JSONs).
If parameters cannot usefully adapt, a natural alternative is to restore the
\emph{features}: align the corrupted test features' first- and second-order
statistics to the clean-training statistics, in the spirit of
NORM~\cite{schneider2020norm} and CORAL~\cite{sun2016coral}. Unlike LN-affine
adaptation, this does reach the space where the corruption shift lives.
Exploratory covariance-CORAL initially looked promising, improving mean
corrupted AUC by roughly $+0.9$ points on three of the four backbones. Held-out
validation dismantled the result: per-channel whitening reorders the
discriminative directions for the SigLIP2 backbones, the sign of the effect
flips across backbones, and a leave-one-condition-out evaluation---the
label-free operating-point selection a deployment would actually face---scored
$-1.62$ points with zero of four backbones positive. No label-free statistical
alignment survived validation. The diagnosis sharpened accordingly: the
failure mode is not ``wrong statistics'' but \emph{rank disruption}---any
transformation that moves features without preserving the score ordering the
frozen head already produces will damage a rank metric as readily as help it.

That diagnosis, together with the two negative results above, pointed at a
narrow opening: do not adapt parameters, do not move features---change
\emph{which modality the frozen fusion trusts}. We explored this through a
six-strategy search (shift-gated blending, channel selection,
projection-space correction, fusion-gate modulation, skeleton-score blending,
and an open-ended track), whose surviving candidate is the
discriminative-reliability reweighting described in Chapter~\ref{ch:03}: a
label-free, tuning-free, per-condition scalar $w$ on the projected visual
stream, driven by the collapse of the visual stream's own solo score
dispersion and gated by a skeleton-shift check, adapting \emph{zero}
parameters. Before adoption it was validated adversarially: the mechanism
uses no labels, reproduces the source model bit-identically when $w=1$, and
its search-time gains reproduced exactly on the production pipeline.

Three distinct aggregations appear in that sentence, and we keep them
explicit because they are easily conflated: $+4.83$ is the cross-backbone
Gaussian-family average, $+8.95$ is one backbone's (SO400M's) Gaussian-family
average, and $+13.2$ is one backbone's single severity-5 condition; none is
interchangeable with the overall 20-condition mean of $+1.21$.

Gaussian noise is exactly the condition family where one modality dies and
the other survives: it most collapses the visual stream's discriminative
signal while leaving the skeleton features untouched (for Gaussian noise and
brightness the skeleton cache is the clean one, reused unchanged;
Chapter~\ref{ch:04}). The gain there is therefore \emph{recovery by routing
to an unaffected modality}, not intrinsic robustness of the fused features.

The zeros in the other three columns require equal care in reading:
\textbf{they are the gate working, not the method failing}. For motion blur
and JPEG compression the skeleton features are re-extracted from degraded
frames, so the skeleton-trust gate $s$ drops to zero and the fusion stays at
the source model---by construction, no rerouting occurs onto a modality that
is itself corrupted, and no harm is done. For brightness shift the visual
stream remains reliable ($w_{\mathrm{vl}} \approx 1$), so there is nothing to
route away from. A reading of these zeros as ``TTA hurts on blur'' would be
wrong twice over: the deltas are zero rather than negative, and they are zero
because the method declines to act outside its safety conditions.

\subsection{Honest caveats}

Four limitations bound the result. First, \textbf{it is rescue, not
robustness}: the mechanism is a routing toward the skeleton stream, so its
benefit is bounded by the skeleton-fusion ceiling, and nothing about the
fused representation becomes intrinsically more corruption-tolerant. Second,
\textbf{the dispersion proxy can over-route}: on one seed, SigLIP2 Base loses
up to $3.2$ points at high-severity Gaussian noise (seed 42, severity 5,
$-3.20$), where its visual scores compress \emph{without} losing rank and the
spread proxy mistakes compression for collapse; that backbone is nonetheless
positive on all three seeds on average ($+1.50\pm1.14$), and this single-seed
regression is the honest price of a label-free reliability estimate. Third,
\textbf{the estimate is transductive}: reliability statistics are pooled over
the whole corruption condition, which assumes batched access to the corrupted
stream rather than streaming operation---standard in the
corruption-robustness setting, but a real gap to online deployment that we
list among future work (Section~\ref{sec:conc-future}). Fourth, \textbf{the
strategy search was scored on the reported test conditions}: the six-strategy
search and finalist comparison (Section~\ref{sec:coral}) were scored on the
same 20 conditions reported in Table~\ref{tab:tta}, so residual
researcher-selection risk remains even though every candidate was label-free
and tuning-free with knobs fixed by stated principles; mitigations: the
leave-one-condition-out check eliminated CORAL, and the adopted method's
gains reproduced on backbones, severities, and seeds unseen during screening.

\section{The LayerNorm Barrier}
\label{sec:ln-barrier}

The TTA results of this chapter are an informative negative finding with
implications beyond violence detection, and they merit a mechanism-level
account.

Entropy-minimization methods such as TENT and SAR were designed for BatchNorm
networks~\cite{ioffe2015batchnorm}, where adaptation recalibrates the running
mean and variance statistics that summarize the source
distribution~\cite{wang2021tent}. That design transfers two assumptions:
that the network carries dataset-level normalization statistics to
recalibrate, and that recalibrating them moves the decision function. Our
fusion head satisfies neither. It normalizes with
LayerNorm~\cite{ba2016layernorm}, which keeps no running
statistics---normalization is computed per sample---so on each batch these
methods can only nudge the LayerNorm affine parameters ($\gamma$, $\beta$;
1{,}536 scalars in our head). With the visual-language backbone, the gate,
and the detection head all frozen, the corruption-induced shift lives
upstream in the frozen features, and affine updates of this size are far too
small to reorder a rank-based AUC---leaving the metric essentially unchanged
($<0.1$ points, Table~\ref{tab:tent-sar-null}).

The two failed alternatives triangulate the barrier. Feature-statistic
restoration (Section~\ref{sec:coral}) shows the problem is not access: NORM/
CORAL-style alignment operates directly in the feature space where the shift
lives, yet degrades AUC because label-free whitening reorders discriminative
directions---rank disruption, with a sign that flips across backbones. The
continual protocol shows the problem is not adaptation volume
either: full-stream continual adaptation leaves the metric unchanged
($<0.1$ points) once the reachable parameters are limited to LN affines. The lesson
is not that the corruptions are benign---Section~\ref{sec:source-degradation}
shows they cost twenty points---but that adaptation confined to LayerNorm
affine parameters cannot move a frozen, rank-based detector.

What does work here is to change \emph{which} modality the frozen fusion
trusts: the label-free discriminative-reliability reweighting routes the
gated fusion toward the corruption-robust skeleton stream when the
visual-language stream's discriminative signal collapses, recovering
corrupted AUC on all four backbones without any parameter updates
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-results}). For frozen LayerNorm-based fusion heads,
this suggests robustness is better pursued through reliability-aware
routing---or, more broadly, through prompt- or adapter-based mechanisms that
alter the features themselves---than through affine test-time adaptation.
The dual-modal architecture is what makes the routing option available at
all: a single-stream frozen detector facing the same barrier would have no
healthy modality to route toward.
